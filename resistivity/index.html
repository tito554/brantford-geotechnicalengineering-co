When the ABEM Terrameter gets unloaded in Brantford, we set up the four-electrode spread across glacial till and outwash plains. The Schlumberger array is our standard for VES here. We inject current, measure potential difference, and the resistivity meter calculates apparent values immediately. Brantford sits on a complex overburden sequence deposited by the Grand River, with Paleozoic bedrock at variable depth. This setup reads contrasts between saturated sand, clay, and limestone. We tie each sounding to a GPS point and log the electrode geometry. Then the raw field data goes through inversion software back at the lab. Our geophysics crew in Brantford has run surveys near the river flats, industrial parcels off Wayne Gretzky Parkway, and residential subdivisions where a MASW profile complements the resistivity cross-section for seismic site class determination.

A 40-meter VES sounding in Brantford's glacial deposits can resolve sand, clay, and bedrock interfaces with a precision of ±2 meters when calibrated to borehole control.

Methodology and scope

In Brantford we often see resistivity signatures jump from 10 ohm-m in wet clay to over 200 ohm-m in dry sand and gravel lenses. That contrast is what makes VES so effective here. The Grand River valley deposits create layered aquitard-aquifer systems that show up clearly on a plotted sounding curve. We run multiple VES points along a profile line, spacing them at 30 to 50 meters depending on target depth. Each sounding reaches 30 to 50 meters below grade with the standard Schlumberger array. Data processing includes smoothness-constrained inversion and removal of cultural noise from buried utilities or overhead power lines. Brantford's mix of 19th-century industrial infill and natural alluvium means we sometimes encounter slag, cinders, or metallic debris that distorts readings. We flag those anomalies in the report and cross-check with historical maps. The final deliverable is a 2D resistivity section showing interpreted layers: topsoil, unsaturated zone, saturated overburden, and bedrock top-of-rock contour. For projects near the Brantford Southern Access Road or new development along Colborne Street, we combine VES with test pit logs to calibrate resistivity values against actual soil descriptions.

Local considerations

Brantford's population of roughly 105,000 relies on municipal wells and the Grand River for water. A poorly sited borehole that punches through a confining clay layer can connect a clean aquifer to surface contamination. That is why skipping a resistivity survey before drilling is a gamble. VES identifies the continuous clay aquitard that protects the lower aquifer. In areas near the old Mohawk Lake industrial district, historical fill contains unknown conductive materials that can mask deeper resistivity targets. We have also mapped leachate plumes from former landfill cells using resistivity contrasts. Brantford's winter freeze-thaw cycle affects topsoil resistivity, so we schedule surveys between April and November for reliable shallow data. The Ontario Ministry of the Environment requires due diligence on groundwater protection, and a VES report forms part of that hydrogeological baseline.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a VES resistivity survey cost in Brantford?

For a typical Brantford site with 4 to 6 VES soundings and a full interpretation report, budget between CA$770 and CA$1,490. The exact cost depends on site access, target depth, and whether we combine resistivity with MASW or test pitting.

What is the difference between VES and ERT?

VES (Vertical Electrical Sounding) gives a 1D resistivity model at a single point — depth versus resistivity. ERT (Electrical Resistivity Tomography) uses multi-electrode cables to produce a continuous 2D cross-section. VES is faster and cheaper for groundwater depth work. ERT gives more lateral detail for complex geology.

How deep can you see with resistivity in Brantford's geology?

With the Schlumberger array we typically reach 40 to 60 meters depth in Brantford. The limit is set by the maximum current we can inject and the resistivity of the glacial sediments. Dry gravel above the water table requires more power to penetrate.

Do I need a resistivity survey before drilling a water well?

Not legally required, but strongly recommended. A VES survey tells you where the saturated sand layers are, how thick they are, and whether a confining clay layer protects the aquifer. It reduces the risk of drilling a dry or low-yield hole.
